# Northstar Summer Lab 2026 — Code of Conduct

> Northstar Summer Lab is fictional. This policy is synthetic content designed to test grounded retrieval and citations.

## Our standard

Northstar Summer Lab is a learning environment built on respect, curiosity, safety, and shared responsibility. The code applies during classes, residence activities, transport operated by the program, online team spaces, social events, and the final showcase.

Participants must:

- Treat others with respect and use the names and pronouns they request.
- Give constructive feedback about work rather than personal attributes.
- Credit collaborators, sources, datasets, and reused code.
- Protect credentials, private conversations, and personal information.
- Follow staff instructions concerning safety, access, and equipment.
- Make reasonable space for different communication and accessibility needs.

## Unacceptable conduct

Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, threats, stalking, unwanted sexual attention, deliberate exclusion, doxxing, retaliation, and unsafe interference with equipment are prohibited. Plagiarism, fabrication of evaluation results, and presenting another team's work as one's own are also prohibited.

Photography or recording requires the agreement of identifiable participants. A showcase media badge does not override an individual's request not to be recorded.

## Reporting a concern

Choose any route that feels appropriate:

- Speak privately with the Conduct Lead.
- Email the Conduct Lead at **conduct@northstar.example**, the confidential address also listed in the Contacts Directory.
- Ask Participant Support to help make a report.
- In an immediate threat, follow the Safety Guide and use the campus emergency number first.

A report should include only what the reporter is comfortable sharing. Helpful details can include what happened, when and where it occurred, who was involved, and any immediate safety need. Anonymous reports are accepted, although limited information may restrict the response.

## Response and non-retaliation

The Conduct Lead acknowledges a named report within one program day and explains the next step. Measures may include a safety plan, schedule or team adjustment, facilitated conversation when appropriate, restricted access, or removal from the program. The response is based on safety and available information; it is not decided by a public vote.

Retaliation against a person who reports, supports a report, or participates in a review is prohibited. Confidentiality is respected as far as possible, but information may be shared with the small number of people needed to address safety or legal duties.

## Everyday disagreements

Not every disagreement is misconduct. Participants should first use clear, respectful conversation or ask a mentor to facilitate when it is safe to do so. A participant never has to confront someone directly before making a conduct report.
